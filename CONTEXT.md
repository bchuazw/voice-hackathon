## Video playbook (distilled from the guide)

### The 5-second rule
Within the first 5 seconds, communicate **exactly what your project does** in one sentence. Not the tech stack, not a greeting. The outcome.

Good openers:
- "I built an AI that turns any podcast into an animated short."
- "This app reads your codebase and writes your docs for you."
- "Watch me clone any voice in real time with one click."

### Hook patterns that work
- **Lead with the outcome** — show finished product first, then explain.
- **Bold claim** — "I replaced my entire customer support team with one API call."
- **Wow moment first** — dramatic before/after; context comes after.

### Recording
- 60–90 seconds, under 60 even better for social
- Bullet-point script, not word-for-word (sound natural)
- Show, don't tell — 80% demo footage
- **Take it outside** — film in real-world scenarios (café, car, kitchen, street). Far more shareable than a desk recording.
- Mention BOTH ElevenLabs and Cursor meaningfully (show the API call / workflow, not just a logo flash)
- 1080p+; vertical 9:16 for Reels/TikTok
- Good audio > good video. Use a decent mic or generate a voiceover with ElevenLabs TTS.

### Editing (CapCut is fine — free)
- Auto captions (most viewers watch muted — captions are essential)
- Bold, centered caption styles with dark background
- Background music at 15–20% volume so voiceover dominates
- Music vibe matches the project (upbeat tech / minimal elegant / etc.)
- Generate music with ElevenLabs Music — describe vibe ("upbeat tech demo, synth, 90 bpm")

## Strategic notes (mine, for the next agent)

- **Sponsor double-tap:** the Cursor judge (Austin) is on the panel. Projects that delight the Cursor team (= things they wish existed) have a multiplier. Anything where Cursor is the *runtime* engine, not just the build tool, scores well.

- **Real-world filming wins.** Repeated three times in the guide. The kitchen / car / café demo beats the laptop demo every time.
